\section{Overview}

This note reports the results from the Stata fixed effects regression in
\texttt{ders- analysis.do}. The analysis uses a simulated balanced panel with
100 units observed annually from 2015 through 2024, for a total of 1,000
observations. The data-generating process is

\begin{equation}
    y_{it} = 1.5x_{it} + \alpha_i + \lambda_t + \varepsilon_{it},
\end{equation}

where $\alpha_i$ is a unit fixed effect, $\lambda_t$ is a time effect, and
$\varepsilon_{it}$ is an idiosyncratic error term.

\section{Specification}

The estimated model is

\begin{equation}
    y_{it} = \beta x_{it} + \lambda_t + \alpha_i + u_{it}.
\end{equation}

The regression is estimated using Stata's \texttt{xtreg, fe} command with year
fixed effects and standard errors clustered by unit:

\begin{verbatim}
xtreg y x i.year, fe vce(cluster id)
\end{verbatim}

\section{Interpretation}

The estimated coefficient on $x$ is 1.5366. Because the simulated true effect is
1.5, the fixed effects estimator recovers the underlying parameter closely. The
coefficient is precisely estimated, with a clustered standard error of 0.0329
and a $p$-value below 0.001.

The within $R^2$ of 0.7257 indicates that the model explains a large share of
within-unit variation in the dependent variable. The estimate is based on
within-unit changes over time, while controlling for time-invariant unit
characteristics and common year effects.

\section{Conclusion}

The Stata fixed effects regression confirms that the panel estimator recovers
the simulated relationship between $x$ and $y$. After accounting for unit fixed
effects, year fixed effects, and clustered standard errors, the estimated effect
of $x$ remains close to the true value used to generate the data.
